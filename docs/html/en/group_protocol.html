Group mode: the protocol and what we measure

English translation of docs/group_protocol.md — the Russian original is the source of truth. Labels inside the diagrams are in Russian.

The prime_lab firmware, module group.cpp: the protocol lives in its own task on the radio core, the compute core works on jobs. A group is a wheel subgroup (letter а–з on the main screen); the server sends the membership and the participants' addresses together with the settings — a new agent joins without reflashing the others. The way is set by gv, the icons are as on the main screen. When to choose which way — modes.md, "Cooperation"; the exchange step by step, slowed down, — the window «↻ Как работает» (how it works), section 2.

1. Numbers from everyone's noise pieces#

  • A piece — the length of the number divided by the number of agents, rounded up to 4 bytes (256 bits: two — 16 bytes, four — 8). Each agent takes the noise from its own sources (randomness.md).
  • A set — K pieces in one PCS frame, for K candidates. One number for all — K = k (default 24); relay and cascade — as many as fit in a frame, up to 64 (256 bits, four — 64).
  • A candidate — one piece from each in random order (the order — from a fast xorshift128+ generator seeded by the RNG for every gathering), the top bit and oddness, sieving by divisors (td): with td 1000, out of 24 candidates about four survive on average.
  • Pieces in advance (pre, only with the RNG alone): the set is ready before it is asked for. One number keeps one set, relay and cascade — up to four; slow sources (CSI, ADC, jitter) gather on request.
  • Two cores (dc 1, relay and cascade): the noise for the pieces is gathered by a producer on the radio core. The relay also assembles and sieves there (16 candidates per pass), the compute core only tests; the cascade sieves on the compute core — the radio core is busy with noise for everyone's numbers.

2. Cascade and relay (gv 2 and 1)#

The number is assembled from everyone's pieces, and one agent tests it — with all bases in a row. Splitting the test of one number does not pay: a composite is rejected by the very first base (≈ 1 ms at 256 bits), while a frame takes longer.

  • Cascade — everyone tests at once, each its own numbers, and each also provides pieces for everyone else's numbers. A prime found goes to the next in order for cross-checking; the turn is not passed.
  • Relay — one holds the turn, the others give it noise. Having found rk primes, it passes the turn and the rest of the reserve of numbers to the next in order (TURN, addressed); that one acknowledges, cross-checks the prime and at once tests its own. One computing stream for the whole group.

The tester's turn:

  1. Gathering round. The request for pieces — to everyone in one broadcast frame (REQ), repeats — addressed to those who did not send. Each replies with a set (PCS).
  2. Numbers. K candidates from everyone's pieces, sieving, the survivors go into the reserve. A new round — while the reserve is less than "survivors per round plus two in reserve"; the cascade keeps the reserve one round ahead (survivors of two rounds plus two).
  3. Testing. The compute core takes packets from the reserve (8 numbers at 256 bits) and tests with all bases until the first prime: a composite — one base, a prime — all of them.
  4. Cross-check — instead of the "one number" agreement. The receiver of the prime tests it with one base the tester did not use (the next after nb: with 8 bases — 23; with 12 there is no cross-check). If it finds a witness, the "prime" is composite: split.
  5. Waiting for pieces (pw). 0 — wait for everyone, re-asking up to 3 times; if a participant still has not sent, it is silent (§ 4). A deadline in ms — after it the missing pieces are replaced by sets of its own noise: the number is still random, it just has less foreign noise in it. If it does not have enough sets of its own for replacement, the agent accumulates them and gives them to its neighbours later. Rounds with replacement and the number of replacements — tr in the grp packet; on the main screen — "pieces from everyone, % of numbers".

3. One number for all (gv 0)#

Every number is tested by everyone; the Miller–Rabin bases are divided round-robin (place in the group, place + N, …): with 8 bases and two agents the first tests 2, 5, 11, 17, the second — 3, 7, 13, 19. At an agent the test stops at the first base that says "composite". A cycle is two messages per agent: the verdict with the number, and the answer.

  1. The number comes with the verdict of the previous cycle (NXT). Everyone at once tests it with their bases and answers the master (RES): whether a witness was found, by which base, how much was computed.
  2. Verdict (NXT). If someone found one — the number is composite, the next master is the finder (if several found one — the choice by a seed from the SHA-256 of the number itself). If nobody found one — the number is prime: the master records it and hands mastership to the next in order. The same message carries the number of the next cycle, and to the new master — also the rest of the reserve (at 256 bits — up to 16 numbers).
  3. Replenishing during testing. When the reserve runs out, the verdict tells everyone "send pieces to the new master" (want): each gives a set at once and starts testing, the master assembles and sieves while everyone tests.
  4. Without a number (start of a session, a break): the master asks for pieces with a request (REQ), sieves and sends out the number itself (NUM).

A group also works with one agent: it is its own master, tests with all bases and sends no protocol frames.

Agreement. Answers about one number must agree: a random composite gives a witness to almost any base (strong pseudoprimes to the first bases do not occur among random 256-bit numbers), so in every cycle either everyone found one or nobody did. The master counts:

  • agr — cycles where everyone's answers were compared;
  • split — not everyone found one: a pseudoprime to someone's bases (the server will re-check) or an error in computing or transmission;
  • dis — in agreement mode (all 1: all bases at each agent, the same order) the first witness bases differ between agents.

The number of the last disagreement and the agents' answers — gls in the grp packet; on the main screen — "agreement %" in the group popup. This is an integrity check of the whole path: there should be no disagreements at all.

The board's other watchdogs (Wi-Fi "zombie", beacon silence) — how_it_works.md, "Temperature and watchdogs".

Waiting time. Pieces and answers are waited for twice the usual time of the slowest participant plus 20 ms (usual — a moving average per participant): an agent throttled by temperature means a delay, not a loss. An answer carries a tag of the number (the low 4 bytes): an answer about another number is not counted.

A silent participant (relay, cascade). Whoever did not send pieces after three requests or did not accept the turn is silent for this agent: the round is assembled without it (its pieces — own noise), the next rounds do not wait for it, the turn and cross-checks bypass it. Any frame from it — and it is waited for again. In the grp packet — mute: [how many times members went silent, mask of the silent places, how many are silent]; on the main screen — "◇" in the group popup, the relay arc to it is dashed.

Rest in turns (relay, cascade; thermal.md, "Rest in turns"). The server sends REST s=… to the hottest of those throttled by the regulator. The agent finishes its turn (relay — until the next one has accepted), tells every participant by address M_REST "resting" — they put it among the silent at once — sets 80 MHz and does not compute; it does not answer group frames, only the pulse goes into the air. After s seconds or on BACK — the frequency from the settings and M_REST "back in action": cascade — its own turns, relay — it waits for the turn (it learns the turn and tester numbers from the very first REQ). The settings number does not change. In the grp packet — rest: [1 resting / 2 will go once it finishes its turn; seconds left; rests in total].

5. Exchange#

  • ESP-NOW on the router's channel, rate nr: 24 Mbit/s — normal; 6 Mbit/s — picks up a signal ~12 dB weaker; 1 Mbit/s — a frame hangs in the air 20 times longer, the transmitter heats the board, and the group waits for the one throttled by the regulator. A protocol frame is up to 600 bytes with the tag W5: frames of another version are not accepted.
  • Group frames are addressed: the radio acknowledges each one and repeats a lost one itself (broadcast frames without acknowledgement get lost). To everyone at once go only the first request for pieces in relay and cascade (a loss is covered by a repeat or the pw deadline), the pulse and absorption frames (scatter.md). The participants' addresses come from the server's registry; the pulse reports a neighbour's address and signal even before the first protocol frame.
  • A frame goes out only after the radio has reported on the previous one: a continuous stream of frames jams the agent's ordinary Wi-Fi (commands, telemetry).
  • The settings number (cid) is the session number: frames of foreign settings are not accepted, so several groups at once do not disturb each other.

A bad transmitter slows everyone. If a group is suddenly slow with many re-asks, look at the pairs (fail_to, rssi_from in the grp packet; the "pairs" row on the old panel /old): the culprit loses frames from itself to any neighbour (2–20 %), frames to it and between the others arrive at 100 %, its telemetry does not go out (udpf) and "not accepted by the radio" grows in etx. Such a board should be replaced (looks like the antenna connector).

6. What we measure#

The counters are in every agent's grp packet; the server combines them by group: the group popup on the main screen (hover over the bus, the ring or the shared number), in detail — the old panel /old.

  • Rate: cycles (turns) and primes per second; the yield of primes against theory — at the testers, as for "each on its own".
  • Comparison with "each on its own": the server remembers how many primes per second each agent gave alone at the same length, bases, sources and sieve, and shows how many times slower the group is.
  • Coherence: the share of numbers from everyone's pieces (tr) and the series of consecutive cycles without a re-ask, a repeat or a break — current and best, in cycles and in time; all the time spent in clean series. For a group — the worst of the participants' series.
  • Cycle time by stage (at the master): waited for pieces, waited for the sieve, testing and answers, verdict to the new master (measured by the previous master: from its verdict to the new one's verdict, minus the length of its cycle, halved); for each stage — how much of it is pure computing, the rest is synchronisation. Relay and cascade write into the same fields: the compute core waited for numbers, noise and sieving on the compute core, testing, passing the turn, the whole turn.
  • Reserve: gathering rounds per cycle, candidates sieved and surviving, in how many cycles a number was waited for, how often the set of pieces was ready in advance.
  • Tests by base: how much the agent tested with each base and how many times it found a witness.
  • Link: delivered and not delivered per participant, the signal from each, the send queue.

  • All checks agree: the yield of primes against theory is within |z| ≤ 2, cross-checks of neighbours' primes — not a single disagreement, the server confirmed every prime in the log, no breaks and no two testers.
  • The cascade is limited by the radio core (noise for the pieces is needed for every candidate, and the RNG gives a word in ~21 µs) and by heat: in 30 s the hottest boards reach 70 °C, the regulator cuts their work to 18–47 %. Without a deadline (pw 0) everyone waits for the throttled board's pieces, with a deadline — not.
  • A cascade of six (90 s): with a 20 ms deadline 60–73 % of the neighbours' pieces are replaced by own noise (for three — 18 %, for a relay of six — 2 %), with 50 ms — 9–13 %, ~100 primes/s. Six plus six absorbers — 104 primes/s; two groups of three at once — 75.
  • The relay shares the turns evenly; a turn takes 27 ms, of which waiting is 17.6 %; 1024 bits — 0.55 primes/s. A participant restarted over USB in the middle of computing drops out within a second (three — ~20 primes/s instead of 38–40) and comes back with its settings after 7 s.
  • Two groups at once do not disturb each other: relays 2 + 2 — 32.9 and 30.3 primes/s; one number 2 + 2 — 216 cycles/s each and 16–17 primes/s, 1 + 3 — 330 and 161 cycles/s.
